WAS ANYTHING LOST IN TRANSLATION?
Things Fall Apart · the Week of Peace violation. Sound on, ideally headphones. The silence is part of the film.

The sound design, scene by scene

0–8s · Intro

Birds, wind, distant voices, hoes striking soil, a thumb-piano melody with a soft flute.

Sound establishes peace before anyone says the word.

8–25s · The violation

The music turns tense and the voices rise. A clay pot shatters, then every sound is cut at once. Silence holds for several seconds.

Silence = shame and judgment: the community says nothing, and that is the verdict.

25–42s · Sound creates tension

Slow, deep drums that swell in volume and speed, over wind, footsteps and muffled village life. Close-ups cut on the beat.

The audience feels that something serious has happened before any explanation.

42–52s · Contrast

The drums are removed without warning. The villagers look, the camera moves slowly, nothing is heard.

Emotion carried by the absence of sound, with no narrator.

52–60s · Conclusion

Faint wind, one bird, a few sparse thumb-piano notes, then black.

Novel → words. Film → sound + silence. The form changes; the emotion remains.
